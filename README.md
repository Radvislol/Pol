# Seimas: Battle for Lithuania

A political-drama strategy game about the **2028 Lithuanian Seimas election**, playable on **phones and desktop**, in **Lithuanian and English** (switch with the LT/EN toggle). You lead a real party and its real leader through a twelve-week campaign, then try to build a 71-seat governing majority and survive the Seimas confidence vote.

> Fictional simulation. Real parties and leaders appear for context; every event, quote and result is invented. Party emblems are original designs, not official logos, and leader portraits are cartoon drawings, not photos or likenesses. Starting polls roughly follow late-2026 averages, and party leaders were checked in November 2026.

## Look and layout

- **Dark "political drama" design**: night-blue glass panels over a blurred landmark, condensed italic headlines on black title bars, amber call-to-action buttons, white answer pills, party-coloured rows, shield emblems, white stat pills and a rolling national-news ticker.
- **Phones**: a compact top bar with the leader's card, stat pills and a campaign timeline (months, debates, endorsements, election day), and a bottom bar with the news ticker and sections.
- **Desktop (1024 px and wider)**: a command-centre layout with a full-width top bar (leader card, timeline, Next week), a navigation rail on the left, the large campaign map in the centre and a docked side panel on the right that shows region details or the latest news. News, debates and voters open as centred cinematic cards. Candidate selection becomes a two-pane screen with a large preview.

## Districts and candidates

- **The real 71 single-member districts.** Names, official numbers and areas follow the boundaries used in the October 2024 election. Each district sits in one of the game's 12 regions plus *Abroad*. A few cross-county districts were placed in the neighbouring region, for example Karšuva (Pagėgiai–Jurbarkas) in Kaunas County and North Nalšia (Ignalina–Visaginas) in Vilnius County.
- **Real incumbents.** In every district, the member actually elected there in 2024 runs again for the party they were elected for (source: Wikipedia, *2024 Lithuanian parliamentary election*). Only facts are shown for real people: name, party, district and year elected. Their name-recognition, charisma and similar scores are labelled as game estimates. They get no invented age, job, biography or scandal risk. Their portraits are generic cartoons, not likenesses.
- **Fictional challengers.** The other candidates are generated for each game:
  - realistic Lithuanian names, with the correct female surname forms;
  - age, profession, years in local politics and two policy priorities;
  - name recognition, charisma, local ties, experience and scandal risk.

  Big parties stand everywhere. Small parties only stand where they have a base. Your own party fields a candidate in every district.
- **Candidates matter.** A district's result is the regional poll × the local lean × the candidate's strength. Strength comes from recognition, charisma and local ties, plus a bonus for incumbents. Star recruits from the region panel raise your candidate's recognition.
- **Where to see it all:**
  - **Map › Districts** (desktop: *71 districts* under the map): every district with its number and leading candidate, your share and the race status. You can search by district, town, number or candidate, and filter by region or by your wins and runoffs.
  - **Region panel:** the region's districts, each opening its district card.
  - **District card:** voters, city share, the 2024 winner, a first-round projection of every candidate and the likely runoff. Two actions for your candidate: local posters and ads (+recognition) or community meetings (+local ties and +ground team).
  - **Candidate profile:** portrait, party, profile and score bars, priorities and projected or final result.
  - **Current Seimas** (Polls › Seats, or the finder): all 141 real members elected in 2024, by party, with district or list position.
  - **New Seimas** (election night and the final screen): all 141 members after your election. District winners are your game's candidates. List seats go to the party leader, then the party's real 2024 list members, then fictional names.

## Party list, district debates and governing

- **Party list (HQ › List).** Order your national list until week 4, when you register it with the VRK; otherwise it is registered automatically in its default order. The pool is:
  - your leader;
  - your party's real 2024 list members;
  - your district candidates, by fame.

  Live scores show the top 10's fame, how many women it has, how many regions it covers and how many experienced members it includes. When you register:
  - a famous top 10 adds buzz;
  - four or more women please teachers, doctors and young voters;
  - every region in the top 10 gets a small boost;
  - experienced members add credibility;
  - dropping sitting MPs from the top 15 costs party unity.
- **Preference votes.** On election night your list is reordered by preference votes, plus a small bonus for list position. A card shows who moved up or down and who takes your list seats. The new Seimas uses this order.
- **Local TV debates.** On any district card you can send your candidate into a one-on-one debate with the strongest rival (1 energy, once per district). There are two local questions, chosen from the region's issues: roads, clinics, jobs, emigration, housing, milk prices, buses and civil defence. Your candidate's charisma counts. Winning adds recognition, local ties and a small regional boost.
- **The first 100 days.** If you form a government:
  - **Swear in a cabinet** for all 14 ministries, with each ministry going to a coalition party. Choose between party colleagues and outside experts. Experts are often more competent but annoy partners. Competence scores are game estimates, and real MPs get a neutral 65.
  - **Take six bills through the Seimas:** Budget 2029, defence at 4% of GDP, a €1,300 minimum wage, a civil partnership law, offshore wind and a progressive income tax. Each party votes according to its positions; you see the expected count first.
  - **For each bill,** put it to a vote, amend it (more votes, half the effect) or withdraw it. Passing bills moves your approval. Forcing partners against their positions, or losing the budget, costs stability.

  Your final stability decides the Prime Minister grade, and the result screen shows a 100-day summary.

## In the Seimas when you don't lead, and candidate scandals

- **Opposition or junior partner.** If you go into opposition or join someone else's government, you vote on the new government's six bills. For each bill you see how every party is expected to vote, and when your seats decide the outcome. Voting in line with your promises raises your rating.
  - As a junior partner, voting against costs the government stability. Voting against your own promises costs party unity. You can also walk out of the coalition, which brings the government down if it drops under 71 seats.
  - In opposition, defeating bills shakes the coalition. Once the government's stability drops below 50 you can call a single no-confidence vote, which needs 71 votes.
  - Bringing the government down earns the special *You brought the government down!* grade, and the result screen summarises your term.
- **Candidate scandals.** From week 3, fictional candidates with a high scandal-risk score can get caught out, for example old posts, unpaid taxes or a viral row. A scandal weakens that candidate in their district and makes the news. When it hits one of yours, you choose:
  - **defend them:** unity up, credibility down, the candidate stays weakened;
  - **apologise:** smaller damage;
  - **replace them:** €10k and 1 favour; a new, less known but clean candidate.

  A lawyer and a press secretary make scandals rarer. Real incumbents are never targeted.

## Finding things

- **Where is it?** The magnifier button in the top bar ("Where is it?" on desktop) lists everything you can do, grouped by section, with a search box. Matching ignores Lithuanian accents, so "kazino" and "pazadai" both work. Tapping an entry jumps straight to that action or opens it.
- **Sections are split into tabs:**
  - **HQ:** Media (ad studio, press conference, nationwide TV, favours, opposition research), Money (fundraising, casino and betting, spending limit), Team (staff) and Promises (reputation, endorsements, changing your platform). Favours, buzz and scandal risk stay visible on every tab.
  - **Polls:** Seats (projection, coalition calculator, possible teams), Polls (national bars, weekly trend, voter groups) and Rivals (dossiers).
  - **Map on phones:** *This week* (message of the week, party task, leader tour, warnings, closest races) and *Regions* (all regions), switched under the map.
  - **Desktop side panel:** *This week* and *News* (headlines and social media).

## The map

- **Zoom and pan.** Use the + and − buttons, double-click, pinch on a phone, or Ctrl/⌘ + scroll on a computer. Once zoomed in, drag to move around. Other buttons show the whole country again or jump to your leader.
- **Hover card (desktop).** Point at a region to see its seats, the top three parties, how close the race is, whether your leader is there, your moves this week and your ground team.
- **Toss-up rings.** Regions where you are within 4 points pulse orange on the *Who leads* and *Battlegrounds* layers.
- **Projected Seimas strip.** Under the map, a 141-seat bar shows every party's projected seats, yours outlined, with the 71-seat line.
- On phones the map comes first, before the weekly plan. Region rows show a short name, a one-line race summary and a bar comparing your share with the leader's.

## More to do

- **Real-calendar events.** Seven public events land on their real dates during the campaign, each in its own region and pulsing on the map that week:
  - the Klaipėda Sea Festival;
  - a Lithuania–Latvia basketball friendly in Kaunas;
  - Žolinė at Pivašiūnai;
  - the Baltic Way anniversary;
  - 1 September;
  - the Vilnius Marathon;
  - the Suvalkija harvest fair.

  Each offers two ways to take part, with their own costs, voter-group effects and sometimes risks, or you can skip it, and a rival takes the stage instead. Bringing your leader to the region first gives +30%. The *This week* panel shows the current event, or the next one so you can plan the bus tour.
- **Election-day turnout drive.** Pressing *Vote!* in week 12 opens a last decision: send volunteers to up to three regions. The closest races are listed first, and bigger ground teams bring out more voters.
- **Campaign chronicle.** The final screen shows your poll line through the campaign and a week-by-week list of the moments that shaped it: debates, news decisions, events, ads, press conferences, opposition research and the turnout drive.
- **Press conferences.** In HQ, take two questions live from reporters at fictional outlets. Answers change buzz, credibility, voter-group moods and sometimes the scandal meter. The effect gets smaller each time you hold one in the same week.
- **Social media.** The News section has a feed of posts reacting to your rallies, ads, door-knocking, polls, reputation, scandals and the latest news, with trending hashtags and an overall mood score.
- **Ad studio.** In HQ, design your own ad:
  - Choose the medium: TV, internet, billboards or radio, each with its own cost and city/countryside reach.
  - Choose the message: one of the five weekly themes. Matching this week's theme gives a bonus.
  - Choose the tone: positive, contrast or attack. Attack ads can backfire.
  - For contrast and attack ads, choose a rival.
  - Choose the audience: one of six voter groups. The ad lands hardest where that group lives.

  A live mock-up (TV screen, phone feed, roadside billboard or radio spot) shows the generated slogan. Each ad airs now and again next week, at most two at a time, and its measured effect is listed in HQ.
- **Rival dossiers.** In Polls, tap a rival for a full profile with these details:
  - poll trend through the campaign;
  - regions they lead;
  - strongest regions;
  - where they stand on the four issues compared with you;
  - last week's moves;
  - how well they would fit in a coalition with you.
- **Coalition calculator.** In Polls, toggle parties to build a team on today's projection. It shows the seat total against 71 and the weakest link between partners.
- **Wide screens.** On desktop, election night and the final result use two columns.
- **Phone menu.** The phone top bar shows the leader's full name, party and week. Language, sound and help are in the ☰ menu.

## Immersion

- **Live LRT debates in full screen.** Each debate is a TV broadcast: an opening title card, the studio with sweeping spotlights, the leaders at their podiums, an "LRT · LIVE" bug with the studio clock and a lower-third caption. The host's question (or a rival's attack) types out on screen, then you pick your answer. Your leader steps forward and delivers it, the audience-reaction line ("worm") climbs or sinks, thumbs and hearts fly up from the crowd, and you hear applause or booing. A rival then replies. At the end the viewers' vote is counted live, with bars filling up and confetti if you win.
- **Week intro.** Each new week opens with a short title sequence ("WEEK 3"), showing which rivals campaigned or attacked you and counting your new poll up from last week's.
- **Animated feedback.** The money, seats and reputation counters count up and down. A campaign move makes a ripple and a floating "+0.8%" (or the rival's loss after an attack ad) appear on the map. Sections fade in, and news, voter and region pictures slowly pan and zoom.
- **Sound.** Short synthesised effects (taps, stings, applause, boos, whooshes and win or loss fanfares) with no audio files. Mute them with the speaker button in the top bar; the setting is remembered. All motion respects the system's "reduce motion" setting.

## Playing

- **Rūta, your campaign manager (optional).** On the party screen, choose *With Rūta* or *Play solo*. With Rūta, she walks you through the first week, explains the rules and gives rough forecasts on region moves. She never picks moves, news answers or debate answers for you. In solo mode there is no tutorial, no tips and no forecasts.
- **No hints on news and debates.** News choices and debate answers show only the words. You find out how they landed afterwards.
- **The campaign map** shows Lithuania's real county outlines. Switch between six layers: who leads, your support, this week's change, battlegrounds, your ground teams and rival moves. Dots around each region show the projected winner of every local district. Markers show your leader, your moves this week, news stories, rivals' attacks and strongholds waiting for a visit. Tap a region to see the race, local intel and every move you can make there.
- **Your leader tours the country.** Rallies are 30% stronger where the leader is and 15% weaker elsewhere. Each week brings one free bus ride to a neighbouring region; longer trips (and flights to the diaspora) cost energy. The week's route is drawn on the map.
- **Plain-language moves.** Each move shows its cost (energy and euros) and its expected effect before you tap.

## Art

Every leader and character has an original cartoon portrait whose expression reacts to the moment: debates, polls, coalition talks, results. Characters include Rūta, the LRT host, the news anchor, the croupier, the bookmaker, the private investigator and a face for each voter group. Regions, news stories and big moments use illustrations of real places: Gediminas Tower, Trakai Island Castle, Kaunas Castle, Klaipėda harbour with the Meridianas, the Hill of Crosses, Aukštaitija's lakes, farmland with a stork's nest, the Seimas and the Presidential Palace. Everything is drawn in SVG inside the component, so there are no image files.

## Strategy layers

- **Game settings.** Three difficulty levels (rival aggression, donations) and four scenarios: Standard election, Energy shock (governing parties start weaker, cost-of-living messages hit harder), Security scare (hawks gain), and Autumn of scandals (more rival scandals, low trust).
- **Message of the week.** Pick one of five messages (cost of living, security, regions, cities and the future, honest politics). Moves in regions where voters care about it are 12% stronger, elsewhere 3% weaker. Sticking with a message builds buzz; dropping one you pushed for weeks looks confused. The choice locks once you campaign that week.
- **Campaign team.** Hire up to three of seven specialists: a strategist (+1 energy), a press secretary, a digital director, a field organiser, a pollster, a fundraiser and an election lawyer. Each has a signing fee and a weekly wage; if you can't pay, someone quits.
- **Party board and unity.** Every week the party board sets a task: gain in a region, raise money, campaign widely, knock on doors, run a clean week, lift the national rating, or place in the debate. Success brings a reward; failure costs unity. Flip-flops, shady deals and drifting from the programme also cost unity. Below 40, a revolt breaks out, and low unity means your own MPs may vote against you as Prime Minister.
- **Rival scandals.** Rivals get into trouble too: hidden debts, poster vandalism, defecting councillors, hot-mic gaffes. You choose whether to attack, stay above it or go through the VRK.
- **Between the rounds.** After round one you see your closest runoffs, sign up to two runoff pacts (their voters back you, yours back them; partners remember it in coalition talks), and send three volunteer teams to the tightest races. Projections update as you plan.
- **Rival dossier.** The polls tab tracks the six biggest rivals: trend, momentum, last week's moves, attacks on you, and how close they are to you politically.
- **Achievements.** Fifteen achievements, saved in the browser across campaigns.

## Consequences

- **Promises and reputation.** Every answer in the news, in debates and on the street becomes a promise. Saying the opposite later is a flip-flop that costs reputation. Reputation (0–100) scales how much good news helps you and how willingly partners join a coalition.
- **Follow-up stories.** Ten decisions come back two or three weeks later: the farmers' petition, the donor behind your silence, landlords' billboards, a fact-check of your pension promise and more.
- **Rivals react.** Parties you hit with attack ads hit back in the same region the next week, and region leaders rush to defend places where you surged.
- **Neglected strongholds slip.** A safe region you ignore for four weeks starts to drift away.
- **Voters stop you.** After rallies and door-knocking, a voter may ask you something directly: a pensioner about heating bills, a farmer about milk prices, a minority parent about schools. Your answer has effects.
- **The weekly recap** lists what your choices led to.

## Systems

- **47 news stories** (including 10 follow-ups, many of them pinned to a place on the map) with three answers each, and **LRT debates** that draw 3 of 5 questions per debate. Midway through every debate a rival attacks you and you choose how to respond. A low reputation invites the "flip-flopper" attack.
- **Polls with margins of error.** Each week's poll comes from one of four fictional polling firms, each with a small house effect, a sample size and a margin of error.
- **Endorsements.** In weeks 6 and 10, trade unions, farmers', business, youth, seniors' and minority organisations back the party their members like most. Winning one gives a boost wherever that group lives.
- **Early voting.** In the final week many people have already voted, so moves are 15% weaker.
- **Star candidates.** Recruit a famous local face to boost one single-mandate district. AI parties field some too.
- **VRK spending limit.** Campaign spending is capped. Going over brings a fine and a scandal.
- **Risky money.** A casino with real European roulette odds, and bets on Žalgiris games. Both lose money on average, and every bet has a 12% chance of leaking to the press.
- **Kompromat.** Pay investigators to dig up dirt on a rival: a 45% chance of a hit and a 20% chance of being exposed yourself.
- **Voter groups.** Pensioners, young people, farmers, business owners, teachers and doctors, and minorities. Each group reacts to your promises, your news choices and your debate answers.
- **Election day.** Weather affects turnout, mostly in rural areas, and the count runs live, followed by the runoffs.
- **Confidence vote.** After the coalition is signed, the Seimas votes on your nomination as Prime Minister. In shaky coalitions, some MPs may rebel.

## The model

- **141 seats, mixed system.** There are 70 proportional list seats, split by Hare–Niemeyer largest remainder among parties with at least 5% of the national vote. The other 71 are single-mandate constituencies, decided by two rounds.
- **Runoffs.** A candidate who wins more than 50% in round one takes the seat. Otherwise the top two go to a runoff. Voters of eliminated parties transfer by ideological distance across four policy axes, and some stay home. There is also anti-landslide tactical voting against a runaway national leader.
- **13 regions.** Map outlines are simplified from the MIT-licensed `@react-map/lithuania` package (see `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`). Vilnius and Kaunas cities are split from their counties, and there is a seat for Lithuanians abroad. Each region has a population, an urban/rural split, local issues, ideal policy positions, and named constituencies with their own leans. For example, the Polish-minority vote is concentrated in Šalčininkai and Naujoji Vilnia.
- **12 playable parties.** Eight are in the Seimas today: LSDP, TS-LKD, Nemuno aušra, DSVL, Liberalų sąjūdis, LVŽS, Laisvės partija and LLRA-KŠS. Four are outsiders below 5%: Darbo partija, Laisvė ir teisingumas, Krikščionių sąjunga and the Greens. Each has its own funds, strongholds and passive trait. AI rivals run their own weekly campaigns.
- **Each week.** You get 6 energy plus donations. Region actions are rallies, online ads, TV/radio, door-to-door canvassing, star candidates and attack ads. You can also shift your promises, raise money, gamble, dig for dirt, and run national actions.
- **Events.** LRT debates happen in weeks 4, 8 and 11. Breaking-news events arrive every one or two weeks, each with three choices, and scandals are triggered by risky fundraising.
- **Election night and coalition talks.** Election night animates the live count, round one and the runoffs. Coalition talks cover willingness, policy demands, political-capital sweeteners, ministry allocation and a stability score. A partner much bigger than you wants the Prime Minister's job, so small parties usually have to grow first or join as junior partners.

## Files

- `src/SeimasGame.jsx`: the whole game as one React component (default export). It uses Tailwind classes and `lucide-react`, and calls no external APIs.
- `dist/seimas.html`: a built, self-contained page. React, ReactDOM and Lucide load from pinned CDN URLs, Tailwind is compiled inline, and fonts come from Google Fonts.
- `build/`: the esbuild + Tailwind build that produces `dist/seimas.html`.

## Build

```sh
npm install
npm run build   # writes dist/seimas.html
```

Progress autosaves in the browser (localStorage), so a campaign can be resumed from the title screen.
